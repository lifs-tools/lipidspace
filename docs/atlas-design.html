LipidSpace / Atlas

Design sketch · engine-side extension

A structural background model for lipidome retrieval

A fixed structure-aware embedding of lipids, a shared vocabulary of structural modules, and a fixed-length fingerprint per lipidome — so any lipidome, from the database or a single upload, can be placed among all the others in one query, incrementally, without recomputing the world.

Owner LipidSpace (C++/Qt + REST) Consumer LipidCompass (ArangoDB) Corpus ~150 datasets · human/mouse · plasma/brain-biased Gate leave-one-dataset-out metadata recovery Status Phase 0 validated — GO

01What this is, in one paragraph

LipidSpace today derives a lipid embedding by PCA over the lipid×lipid Tanimoto distance matrix of the current analysis (compute_global_distance_matrix, cols_for_pca = 7), compares lipidomes by Hausdorff distance between the resulting point clouds, and clusters them. That is accurate but batch: adding one lipidome can shift the PCA frame, invalidating every coordinate, every pairwise Hausdorff distance, and the dendrogram — the O(L²)+O(N²) recompute you already know as "exceedingly slow, not incremental."

The Atlas keeps LipidSpace's structural lipid distance but freezes the coordinate frame once over a reference lipid universe, defines a shared vocabulary of K structural modules in that frame, and represents each lipidome as a fixed-length compositional fingerprint over those modules. Fingerprints are independent of one another, so placing a new lipidome costs the size of that one lipidome plus an index lookup — never a global recompute. Hausdorff is retained as an exact re-ranking step over a short candidate list, not as the primary index.

Recommendation

Build it entirely inside LipidSpace and expose it over the existing REST service. LipidSpace owns every primitive this needs — cppgoslin parsing, the Tanimoto distance, the Lanczos PCA, the Hausdorff kernel. Keeping it here yields a self-contained, publishable feature and avoids a LipidSpace→LipidCompass dependency. LipidCompass becomes a consumer: it persists fingerprints and metadata, and calls the Atlas endpoints.

Decisions recorded in this revision

Lipid distance = LipidSpace Tanimoto. Frame & module vocabulary built from SwissLipids as structural support; resolution and weighting calibrated from observed data as the measure. K is an output of class-stratified adaptive splitting to a resolution ε. Class-based module distinction accepted biochemically, with a pathway/hop-distance correction noted for later. Cadence separates frequent incremental adds from rare versioned refits. The validation gate is leave-one-dataset-out on the ~150-dataset corpus. Full ledger in §12.

04Modules & the fingerprint

The module vocabulary and its resolution

Modules are the shared codebook: K structural neighbourhoods in the frozen frame, computed once. Two data sources play distinct, non-interchangeable roles.

  • Support — SwissLipids + LIPID MAPS COMP_DB. Build the frame and modules over the union of two enumerated combinatorial databases, so coverage spans structural space evenly and a new upload rarely falls outside it. LIPID MAPS COMP_DB (a flat TSV of 64,891 species across 163 classes: headgroup, abbrev, mass, formula, chain_type, generated from commonly occurring acyl/alkyl chains) is the biologically-tempered core — the common-chain basis bakes the "ignore rare combinations" principle into the support itself, not only into the frequency weighting — while SwissLipids adds breadth and finer isomeric resolution. Harmonize their class systems via goslin and bring both to a consistent resolution (species). Because each database's per-class species count ≈ that class's combinatorial isomer count, adaptive splitting hands each class modules in proportion to its structural richness — a triacylglycerol region earns many, a glycosphingolipid region few — with no hand-tuned per-class formula.
  • Measure — observed data (Lipidome Projector reference + the LipidCompass corpus). Calibrate resolution here. Weight the SwissLipids points by observed frequency so rare enumerated combinations stay in the support (a query lipid can still land there) but never force a spurious module. Temper the weight — presence across datasets rather than raw abundance, or frequencyα with a uniform floor — so resolution follows real data without collapsing in sparse regions; sweep the temper in Phase 0.

K is an output, not an input. Pick a target within-module structural spread ε and let each class split (x-means / g-means, or a hierarchical cut at height ε) until it reaches ε. The parameter-derived complexity score — chain count, carbon / double-bond / hydroxylation ranges, bond-type and branching modifiers, all already parsed by cppgoslin — serves as a floor for under-measured-but-combinatorially-rich classes and as a diagnostic: a class that is theoretically rich yet earns few modules is flagging under-sampling in the reference set. COMP_DB tabulates this score directly as its per-class species count — TG 10,480 ≫ the 2-chain glycerophospholipids at ~2,864 each ≫ complex glycosphingolipids at ~8 — so the floor is looked up, not estimated.

From a lipidome to a vector

For a lipidome with lipids lᵢ and normalized abundances aᵢ, and modules k = 1..K:

  • Hard assignment: f[k] = Σ aᵢ over lipids whose nearest centroid is k.
  • Soft assignment (preferred): f[k] = Σ aᵢ · wᵢₖ, where wᵢₖ is a softmax over distances to the nearest few centroids. This blunts the boundary sensitivity that otherwise makes results swing with K.
  • Normalize compositionally: scale f to sum to one; optionally centre-log-ratio transform so downstream cosine/Euclidean respects that the data is a composition, not counts.

The result is a length-K vector, identical in shape for every lipidome regardless of which lipids it contains. Two lipidomes that share no identical species but distribute their mass into the same structural modules land close together — the structure-awareness that plain name overlap cannot give you.

Two units: sample and dataset

A dataset holds up to several hundred samples measured on one instrument and protocol, across one or more batches (consecutive MS runs), spanning treatments or sample groups, sometimes replicated. Fingerprint at the sample level for fine placement and aggregate to the dataset level for the background map. Collapse technical replicates before fingerprinting so they do not inflate apparent density. Multiple samples per dataset also make per-dataset eigenlipids concretely computable — see §08.

Abundance, done right

LipidSpace currently injects intensity as an extra Hausdorff coordinate (add_column(PCA_intensities)). In the fingerprint, abundance is the histogram weight — its natural role — so quantitative and structural information combine cleanly instead of competing on one appended axis.

05The "fit a new lipidome" operation

Database entry or user upload — the path is identical, at the chosen unit (sample or dataset aggregate, §04). Steps 1–2 already exist in LipidSpace; only the middle is new.

  1. Ingest & parse. Read the lipidome from mzTab-M via the existing load_mzTabM import; parse names with cppgoslin at any nomenclature level.
  2. Project into the frozen frame. Known lipids read from the coordinate table; unseen lipids get Nyström-projected from their Tanimoto distances to the reference set, then cached.
  3. Compute the fingerprint. Soft-assign to modules, weight by normalized abundance, normalize.
  4. Retrieve neighbours. Cosine / Jensen–Shannon / Bray–Curtis against the reference fingerprints; brute-force now, ANN when the corpus is large.
  5. Re-rank exactly (optional). Run the existing Hausdorff on the top-k candidates only, in the frozen frame — the validated measure, at O(k) not O(N).
  6. Place & report. Project into the dataset-level map; return neighbours, distances, metadata, coordinates, and a confidence (distance threshold or permutation p-value) so out-of-distribution uploads say "no confident match." With this corpus that confidence is required, not optional — see §10.

08The Eigenlipid bridge

Eigenlipids (WGCNA for lipids) group lipids into modules by co-abundance correlation across samples and summarize each with its first principal component. They overlap with this design on the "modules → fixed-length vector" mechanic but differ on the axis that matters: co-abundance modules are dataset-dependent and need many samples, so they cannot form a transferable background or fingerprint a single lipidome. Structure modules can. The two are complementary, and the design absorbs the eigenlipid idea rather than competing with it:

  • Structure-defined modules, eigenlipid summaries. Keep the fixed structural modules as the vocabulary; for datasets with replicates, additionally summarize each module by an eigenlipid (first PC of that module's abundance submatrix). This is now concretely feasible, not hypothetical — every dataset carries up to several hundred samples on a single protocol, exactly the within-dataset replication an eigenlipid needs. Where a dataset spans several batches, block on or regress out batch first so a module's first PC captures biology, not run-to-run drift. Single-sample queries fall back to the histogram.
  • Two similarity lenses. Report structural/compositional similarity (fingerprint distance) and functional similarity (module preservation) side by side; disagreement is informative.
  • A stated open problem. The 2026 review flags module transferability as a limitation and asks for new eigenlipid-generation strategies. Structure-informed module definition is a credible answer — a clean framing for the standalone LipidSpace publication.
Naming

Adopt the field's vocabulary: call the structural regions modules. It aligns with what lipidomics readers already know and makes the eigenlipid relationship explicit rather than incidental.

09Operations & reproducibility

Add vs refit — two cadences, not one

The few-day cadence is adding data, not refitting the vocabulary. Adding a dataset: parse it, Nyström-project any new lipids into the existing frozen frame, assign to existing modules, fingerprint, insert into the index — cheap, incremental, and fingerprints stay comparable to everything already stored. Refitting the frame and modules (re-run Tanimoto + PCA + splitting over the grown universe) is a rare, deliberate version bump that re-fingerprints the whole corpus — only O(N·size), linear, but it breaks cross-version comparability, so reserve it for a slow cadence or a coverage-triggered event.

Determinism

A database and a paper both need deterministic results. Seed the splitting and any randomized projection; seed the Hausdorff sampler (compute_hausdorff_distance currently starts from rand()) if it is used in the re-rank. Stamp every result with the atlas version id, and treat a frame refit as a deliberate, announced version bump — never a silent side effect of new data.

10Phase 0 — corpus, protocol, gate

The GO/NO-GO. It runs in Python, offline, against the existing corpus and the Hausdorff output you already trust — before any C++ is written.

The corpus

~150 datasets; each is one instrument and protocol carrying up to several hundred samples across one or more batches (consecutive MS runs) and across treatments or groups, sometimes replicated — and batch labels are only sometimes recorded. It is biased: human and mouse dominate species, blood plasma and brain dominate tissue. It is usable as a background provided the confidence handling and imbalance-aware validation below — and note the geometry itself is insulated from this bias because the frame and modules come from SwissLipids, not from observed frequency.

All ~150 datasets are available as mzTab-M, which LipidSpace already imports (load_mzTabM) for lipid names and per-assay quantities — so the ingest path exists on day one. mzTab-M's ms_run metadata is the natural source for the batch labels where they exist, and its study_variable entries carry the treatment/group structure; extending the current basic import to capture both is small and well-scoped.

Nested technical structure: platform, batch, sample

The technical hierarchy is dataset (one instrument and protocol) ⊃ batch (consecutive MS runs) ⊃ sample, and batch labels are only sometimes recorded. Two technical effects live at different levels. The platform/protocol effect is dataset-aligned — constant within a dataset, so perfectly confounded with dataset identity and not separable by design. The batch effect varies between runs within a dataset; where labelled it can be corrected before fingerprinting, and where not it is absorbed by compositional normalization and, for the primary gate, dwarfed by the plasma-vs-brain contrast. The central Phase 0 diagnostic remains cross-dataset tissue coherence: plasma from lab A should sit with plasma from lab B, not with brain from lab A. If dataset/platform identity dominates instead, the technical signal is winning, and compositional normalization plus a shared lipid-class vocabulary are needed before anything else.

Protocol — leave-one-dataset-out, never leave-one-sample-out

Hold out an entire dataset (all its samples), fingerprint it fresh against the remaining 149, and check whether its neighbours share its tissue and species. Holding out whole datasets is mandatory: samples within a dataset share the platform (and often a batch), so leave-one-sample-out would leak that technical signature and inflate the score. LODO reproduces the production "fit a new lipidome" operation exactly, and 150 datasets support it.

Metrics — imbalance-robust, strong contrast first

Aggregate accuracy is inflated by the human/plasma majority. Use balanced accuracy, macro-F1, and per-class recall, and report minority cells separately as under-powered. Make the primary gate the strong, well-populated contrast — tissue: plasma vs brain, whose lipid profiles are biochemically very different. Treat human-vs-mouse as a weak contrast (both mammals) and secondary.

Confidence is required, not optional

A biased background returns a confidently wrong nearest neighbour for any query unlike anything it holds — a plant, a rare tissue. Calibrate a distance-threshold / permutation OOD score in Phase 0 so "where does it fit" can answer "nothing close enough."

Read Hausdorff as a check, the fingerprint as a floor

Compute reference Hausdorff in the frozen frame and use agreement (Mantel / ARI) only as a consistency cross-check — it is a data-dependent, outlier-dominated, abundance-as-dimension measure, so disagreement may mean the fingerprint is behaving better. Metadata recovery via LODO is the gate. And structural-only fingerprints are a deliberate lower bound (the pathway term is deferred, §12), so read the numbers as a floor, not a ceiling.

Sweep, and require a plateau

Sweep ε, frame dimensionality d (7 was tuned for a single analysis; a support-scale frame likely needs more), hard-vs-soft assignment, the frequency temper, and the distance metric, and confirm the frame and modules are stable whether built on COMP_DB, SwissLipids, or their union — cross-database agreement is itself a robustness signal. Commit to an implementation only where results sit on a stable plateau, not a knife-edge optimum. If no plateau emerges, the structural fingerprint alone is insufficient and the pathway correction moves up the roadmap.

First-run evidence — GO

Run on the live corpus: 179 datasets (188 mzTab-M files, unit-harmonized to per-sample relative composition), one LipidSpace /pca call for the 2D frame and the 179×179 Hausdorff, then k-means modules + compositional fingerprints under leave-one-dataset-out. The fingerprint recovers tissue and species and separates the strong contrasts at least as well as LipidSpace's own Hausdorff, on a stable plateau (K = 20–50, soft assignment, JSD / Bray–Curtis).

Dimensionality tested — and ruled out

We added a PcaDimensions parameter to /pca and re-ran in 7D: results are unchanged (tissue p@5 0.29–0.35, plasma/brain 0.70–0.87), because 83% of the frame's variance lives in the first two PCs and the Hausdorff ceiling was already computed internally in 7D. So dimensionality is not the ceiling — the remaining levers are class-stratified modules, explicit batch handling, and a richer lipid distance. Mantel agreement stays weak (0.11–0.32) because the fingerprint captures biology the Hausdorff misses. Full numbers and scripts in docs/atlas-phase0/.
